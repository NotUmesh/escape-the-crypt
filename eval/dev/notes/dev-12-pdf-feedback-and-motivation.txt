FEEDBACK AND MOTIVATION

Feedback is information about how a learner's work compares with a
goal. Researchers separate it into two kinds. Task feedback comments
on the work itself, for example by pointing out a missing step in an
argument. Person feedback comments on the learner, for example "you
are clever", and it says little about how to im-
prove.

TWO KINDS OF REWARD

A reward that comes from outside the task, such as money or a grade,
is called an extrinsic reward. A reward that comes from the task
itself, such as curiosity or enjoyment, is called an intrinsic
reward. A well-known finding is that heavy extrinsic rewards can
reduce interest in a task that learners already enjoy.

Praise that is specific tends to support self-esteem better than
praise that is vague. A learner who hears "your second paragraph
gives a clear example" learns what to repeat, while a learner who
hears "good job" learns only that the teacher is pleased.
