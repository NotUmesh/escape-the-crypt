Layers of the atmosphere

Earth's atmosphere is a layer of gases held in place by gravity. Dry air is about 78 percent nitrogen and about 21 percent oxygen. Argon makes up most of the remaining 1 percent.

Scientists divide the atmosphere into five layers. Starting at the ground and going up, they are the troposphere, the stratosphere, the mesosphere, the thermosphere and the exosphere. The lithosphere is not a layer of the atmosphere: it is the rocky outer shell of the Earth itself.

Each layer is known for something different. The troposphere is where almost all weather happens. The stratosphere contains the ozone layer, which absorbs harmful ultraviolet light from the Sun. The mesosphere is where most meteors burn up. The thermosphere is where auroras appear and where the International Space Station orbits.

The troposphere is about 12 kilometres thick on average, thinner at the poles and thicker at the equator. In the troposphere, the air usually gets colder as you go higher.

The exosphere is the outermost layer, where the atmosphere gradually fades into space.
