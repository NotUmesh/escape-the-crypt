SLEEP AND MEMORY

Sleep is a well-known aid to learning, although the reasons are still
being studied. During sleep the brain replays recent experi-
ences, and this replay is thought to help move new information into
long-term storage. The process is called memory consolidation.
Researchers who study consolidation often compare sleep-deprived
volunteers with rested ones.

STAGES OF SLEEP

A night of sleep moves through four stages in a fixed order. The
stages are:

1. Light sleep, when the body begins to relax.
2. Deeper sleep, when heart rate and breathing slow down.
3. Slow-wave sleep, the deepest stage, when the brain is hardest to
   wake.
4. REM sleep, when most vivid dreams occur.

WHY IT MATTERS

Studies of students suggest that self-esteem and mood also depend on
sleep. In one well-known experiment, volun-
teers who slept for eight hours recalled more word pairs than volun-
teers who stayed awake. The full results appear on pp. 5-
10 of the lab report.

The effect is strongest for facts that were studied just before bed.
Adults need about seven to nine hours of sleep, while teenagers need
about eight to ten hours.
