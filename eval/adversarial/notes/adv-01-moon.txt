The Moon and its phases

The Moon does not make its own light. It shines because it reflects light from the Sun. At every moment, half of the Moon is lit by the Sun and half is in darkness.

The phases of the Moon happen because we see different amounts of the lit half as the Moon orbits Earth. When the lit half points away from us, we see a new moon. When most of the side facing Earth is lit, we see a gibbous moon, and when the whole of the side facing Earth is lit, we see a full moon.

The Moon takes about 27 days to orbit Earth. A full cycle of phases, from one new moon to the next, takes about 29.5 days, because Earth is also moving around the Sun.

The Moon always shows the same face to Earth, because it spins once on its axis in the same time it takes to orbit once. This is called tidal locking.

One orbit of the Moon around Earth takes about 35 days.
